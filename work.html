Our Work in the Field

Clearing the Etirae. Creating livelihoods. Rebuilding peace. One acre at a time.

How We Work

We don't just cut down trees and leave. TULRI employs a community-led, zero-waste model that addresses the root causes of land degradation while creating immediate economic and security benefits for the people of Turkana West.

01
🌳

Land Restoration & Grazing

Our community clearing crews manually uproot Prosopis from critical zones like Natamakarwo and the Nanam River corridor. We then reseed with indigenous grasses to restore pastoralist livelihoods and reopen traditional grazing routes.

Read Project Details →
02
🔥

Sustainable Livelihoods

We train women and youth to transform cleared biomass into value-added products: smokeless eco-briquettes for clean cooking, termite-resistant fencing poles, and heat-treated, seed-destroyed livestock feed for dry-season survival.

Read Project Details →
03
🤝

Cross-Border Peacebuilding

By reopening traditional migratory routes and shared water points, we reduce resource bottlenecks that drive livestock raiding. We facilitate eco-dialogue forums among border communities to foster lasting peace.

Read Project Details →
04
📢

Grassroots Advocacy

We translate community data into actionable policy briefs for the Turkana County Government and KeNHA, advocating for invasive species management as a matter of public safety and economic security.

Our Strategic Targeting Framework

We don't clear randomly. TULRI prioritizes interventions based on where Prosopis causes the greatest combination of livelihood, water, grazing, and security challenges.

Very High Priority

🐄 Grazing Lands

Areas where Prosopis has displaced pasture, restricted livestock movement, and threatened critical dry-season grazing reserves.

💧 Water Points

Boreholes, shallow wells, and riverine areas (like the Nanam/Tarach Rivers) where dense thickets block community and livestock access.

🛤️ Livestock Corridors

Traditional migratory routes connecting settlements to grazing areas, ensuring safe and unobstructed movement to prevent cross-border friction.

High Priority

🛣️ Roads & Access Routes

Corridors connecting villages, markets, and health facilities where thickets narrow access, reduce visibility, and create safety hazards.

🏘️ Settlements

Homesteads, paths, and communal spaces where encroaching thorny thickets restrict daily movement and compromise community safety.

🌾 Agricultural Areas

Existing farms and zones with agroforestry potential where Prosopis is actively competing with crops and reducing productive land.

Medium Priority

🏫 Schools & Public Facilities

School compounds, health facilities, and markets. These serve as high-visibility demonstration sites for immediate community engagement.

🌱 Degraded Restoration Sites

Areas with dense, continuous stands where native vegetation has declined. Targeted for long-term ecological restoration demonstrations.

How We Turn a Threat into Treasure

We don't just cut and burn. We use every part of the Etirae to rebuild our community.

1

Clear

Our community crews manually clear thickets from grazing lands, water points, and roads, protecting native acacia trees.

➔
2

Transform

Wood is carbonized into smokeless eco-briquettes. Pods are heat-treated to create safe, seed-destroyed livestock feed.

➔
3

Sustain

Products are sold locally. Profits fund more clearing, create green jobs for youth, and restore peace to our rangelands.

✓ No Open Burning: Zero air pollution or wildfire risk.
✓ Seed Destruction: Heat treatment prevents re-spread.
✓ Native Tree Protection: Prosopis poles replace acacia cutting.
