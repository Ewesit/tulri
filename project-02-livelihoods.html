Eco-Briquettes & Safe Feed Production

Transforming a destructive invasive weed into sustainable income and safe drought fodder.

Project CodeTL-2026-002
LocationTurkana West Sub-County
StatusActive

The Challenge

While Prosopis is an ecological threat, communities are forced to rely on it for emergency firewood, charcoal, and livestock fodder during severe droughts. However, burning it produces heavy smoke, and feeding raw pods to livestock spreads viable seeds across the landscape through dung, accelerating the invasion.

Our Intervention

TULRI turns this liability into a community asset through circular bioeconomy solutions:

  • Smokeless Eco-Briquettes: Training women and youth groups to carbonize and compress cleared Prosopis wood into high-efficiency, smokeless fuel, providing a cleaner alternative to traditional charcoal and a new income stream.
  • Safe Livestock Feed Blocks: Milling and heat-treating Prosopis pods to create nutritious drought feed. Crucially, the heat treatment destroys the seeds, ensuring that feeding livestock does not plant more Prosopis in the pasture.
"We used to burn Prosopis and cough all night. Now, we make briquettes. It burns longer, there is no smoke, and we sell the extra to buy maize for our children."
— Akai, Women’s Eco-Briquette Group Leader, Lokichoggio

Verified Outcomes

Impact Metrics (2026 Baseline)

200+ Households trained in briquette & feed production
100% Seed destruction rate in treated feed blocks
30% Average increase in household income for participating women

The Challenge

In Turkana West, the unchecked invasion of Prosopis juliflora (Etirae) has choked traditional grazing plains like Natamakarwo and blocked access to the Tarach River. This ecological bottleneck forces pastoralist communities into congested, contested spaces, drying up shallow wells and destroying indigenous grass biodiversity.

Our Intervention

TULRI employs a community-led, ecologically sound approach to land reclamation:

  • Manual & Mechanical Clearing: Removing dense thickets from critical water points, highway corridors, and grazing zones, ensuring root destruction to prevent aggressive coppicing.
  • Indigenous Reseeding: Distributing and protecting native grass seeds (e.g., Cenchrus ciliaris, Eragrostis) to accelerate natural pasture recovery.
  • Participatory Mapping: Working with local grazing committees to identify and prioritize the most ecologically and economically vital zones for immediate restoration.

What Happens to Cleared Prosopis?

TULRI follows a zero-waste, circular bioeconomy model. Every part of the cleared Prosopis tree is utilized to maximize community benefit and environmental restoration:

Eco-Briquettes

Dense Prosopis wood is carbonized and compressed into smokeless, long-burning fuel. Provides clean energy and income for women's groups.

Termite-Resistant Fencing

Thick trunks are cut into durable poles for bomas, school fences, and granaries. Replaces the need to cut native acacia trees.

Safe Livestock Feed

Pods are harvested, milled, and heat-treated to destroy seeds. Creates nutritious drought fodder without spreading Prosopis.

🚫 What We DON'T Do: Open Burning

Unlike conventional clearing methods, TULRI never open-burns Prosopis. Burning wastes valuable resources, creates air pollution, risks wildfires, and can actually spread seeds. Our value-addition approach ensures every cleared tree contributes to community resilience and environmental restoration.

"Before TULRI came, the river path was gone. We couldn't see the water, and the thickets hid dangers. Now, our goats can reach the river, and we can see the horizon again."
— Lokwang'ae, Community Elder & Grazing Committee Member, Turkana West

Verified Outcomes

Impact Metrics (2026 Baseline)

500+ Hectares of rangeland cleared and reseeded
12 Critical water points reopened to livestock
85% Reduction in Prosopis regrowth in treated zones
