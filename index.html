Why We Exist

For generations, the rangelands of Turkana West sustained our people. Then Prosopis juliflora—the Etirae—came.

It choked the Nanam River. It consumed the plains of Natamakarwo. It created blind spots on our highways where accidents happen and criminals hide. It forced our livestock into contested border areas, sparking conflicts.

TULRI exists because we refused to watch our land die.

We are not an outside organization. We are sons and daughters of Turkana. We clear the Etirae with our own hands. We train our women and youth to turn it into briquettes and safe livestock feed. We reopen the grazing routes that connect our communities.

This is not a project. This is our home.

Where We Work

We target four critical zones where the Etirae is causing the most damage to our livelihoods, safety, and peace.

🐄

Grazing Lands

The plains of Natamakarwo used to feed thousands of cattle. Now they're choked with thorns. We're clearing and reseeding native grasses so our herds can graze safely again.

💧

Water Points

Women and children walk hours to fetch water, only to find the Nanam River access blocked by dense thickets. We're clearing paths so water flows freely again.

🛣️

Roads & Highways

Dense bushes along the Lokichoggio-Kakuma highway create deadly blind spots and hiding places for criminals. We're working with KeNHA to make our roads safe.

🌾

Farms & Croplands

Agro-pastoralist families are losing their crops to Etirae invasion. We're protecting farmland and teaching sustainable land management so families can feed themselves.

How We Turn a Threat into Treasure

We don't just cut and burn. We use every part of the Etirae to rebuild our community.

1

Clear

Our community crews manually clear thickets from grazing lands, water points, and roads, protecting native acacia trees.

➔
2

Transform

Wood is carbonized into smokeless eco-briquettes. Pods are heat-treated to create safe, seed-destroyed livestock feed.

➔
3

Sustain

Products are sold locally. Profits fund more clearing, create green jobs for youth, and restore peace to our rangelands.

"Before Etirae took over, we could see the horizon. Now, the river path is gone, and our sons are forced to migrate into contested areas just to find grass. Clearing this weed is not just about land. It is the only way to bring back our peace."
— Lotubon, an elder herdsman from Moruangitak, Turkana West.
