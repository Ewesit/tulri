The Etirae Crisis

More than a weed. A direct threat to our land, our safety, our livelihoods, and our peace.

An Ecological and Human Emergency

The unchecked invasion of Prosopis juliflora is no longer just an environmental issue in Turkana West. It is a catalyst for road accidents, a hiding place for criminals, a destroyer of prime grazing lands, and a primary driver of cross-border conflict.

🌳

Choking Our Grazing Lands

Prosopis has completely consumed vast, fertile plains like Natamakarwo. It outcompetes indigenous grasses, leaving livestock with nothing to eat and forcing herders to travel further into dangerous, contested territories.

🛣️

Highway Blind Spots & Insecurity

Dense thickets along the Lokichoggio-Kakuma highway create deadly blind spots, causing vehicles to hit animals and pedestrians. Worse, these impenetrable bushes act as hideouts for criminals and armed groups, making daily travel a severe risk.

🐄

The Livelihood & Health Trap

Livestock suffer from severe jaw decay and thorn punctures. Meanwhile, women and children spend extra hours every day navigating dangerous thickets just to fetch water from the Nanam River, facing risks of thorn infections and sap-related eye injuries.

🤝

Resource Bottlenecks & Conflict

As fertile lands and water points disappear under the thorns, pastoralists are forced into smaller, contested areas. This artificial resource bottleneck is a primary driver of livestock raiding and cross-border friction in the region.

⚠️ The Tipping Point

"Prosopis grows everywhere. If left uncontrolled, it has the potential to cover every single inch of usable space in Turkana West. We are running out of time to reclaim our land."

This is not a future threat. It is happening today. Without immediate, community-led intervention, the rangelands that have sustained our people for generations will be lost forever.

Join the Restoration Effort
"Before the thickets took over, we could see the horizon. Now, the river path is gone, the roads are dangerous, and our sons are forced to migrate into contested areas just to find grass. Clearing this weed is the only way to bring back our peace."
— Lotubon, an elder herdsman from Moruangitak, Turkana West.

We Have the Solution

TULRI is not just identifying the problem; we are fixing it. Through circular bioeconomy, community clearing, and eco-peacebuilding, we are turning this threat into treasure.
