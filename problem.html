The Prosopis Crisis

More than a weed. A direct threat to our land, our safety, our livelihoods, and our peace.

An Ecological and Human Emergency

The unchecked invasion of Prosopis juliflora (locally known as Etirae) is no longer just an environmental issue in Turkana West. It is a catalyst for road accidents, a hiding place for criminals, a destroyer of prime grazing lands, and a driver of cross-border conflict.

🌳

Ecological Devastation

Prosopis has completely consumed vast, fertile grazing plains like Natamakarwo. It blocks access to the Tarach River, dries up shallow wells by consuming excessive groundwater, and outcompetes indigenous grasses, leaving livestock with nothing to eat.

🛣️

Road Safety & Security

Dense thickets along highways create deadly blind spots, causing vehicles and motorbikes to hit animals, children, and unaware road users. Furthermore, these impenetrable bushes act as hideouts for criminals and armed groups during conflicts, making daily travel a risk.

The Livelihood Paradox

Livestock suffer from tooth decay and severe thorn punctures. While communities are forced to use Prosopis for charcoal, firewood, and fencing, this reliance spreads the seeds further. Women and children spend extra hours every day navigating thickets just to fetch water.

Cross-Border Tensions

As fertile lands and water points disappear, pastoralists are forced into smaller, contested territories. This artificial resource bottleneck is a primary driver of livestock raiding and cross-border friction along the Kenya, Uganda, and South Sudan corridors.

⚠️ The Tipping Point

"Prosopis grows everywhere. If left uncontrolled, it has the potential to cover every single inch of usable space in Turkana West. We are running out of time to reclaim our land."

This is not a future threat. It is happening today. Without immediate, community-led intervention, the rangelands that have sustained our people for generations will be lost forever.

TULRI is not just identifying the problem; we are fixing it. Through circular bioeconomy, community clearing, and eco-peacebuilding, we are turning this threat into treasure.
